Background

I am an oral and maxillofacial pathologist with over fifteen years of clinical exposure in general dentistry and diagnostic pathology, and eleven years teaching undergraduate and postgraduate students.

Alongside clinical and teaching work, I have spent the last several years in institutional quality and accreditation, serving as IQAC–NAAC Coordinator and departmental ISO coordinator, which means defining criteria, aligning stakeholders across departments, and building evidence that stands up to external audit.

My research sits at the intersection of oral oncology and technology: molecular diagnostics, oral cancer biomarkers, and the application of artificial intelligence to oncology. On that work I am the clinical domain expert rather than the modeller. I define the diagnostic reference standard, do the annotation, and read model failures clinically. It is the part that decides whether the output means anything.

Those two threads converge. Institutional Ethics Committee work, consent and data stewardship in my own studies, and the audit machinery of NAAC and ISO turn out to be the same machinery that emerging AI governance frameworks are asking healthcare organisations for. That convergence is where I want to spend the next phase of my work. It is set out in more detail here.
